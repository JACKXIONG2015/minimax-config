# Industry Prioritization Matrix

Not all 12 dimensions carry equal weight. Before kicking off research, pick the
industry archetype and read the high-priority / lower-priority lists to plan
where to spend time.

## Archetypes

| Industry Type | High-Priority Dimensions | Lower-Priority Dimensions |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-border e-commerce | Customers, Supply Chain, Sales Platforms & Social, Product & Pricing | Technology & IP |
| B2B manufacturing | Supply Chain, Financial & Capital, Technology & IP | Social Media |
| SaaS / Tech | Technology & IP, Financial & Capital, Product & Pricing | Supply Chain |
| Consumer goods / DTC | Brand & Marketing, Channels & Markets, Product & Pricing | Technology & IP |
| Traditional retail | Channels & Markets, Supply Chain, Financial & Capital | Technology & IP |
| Financial services | Compliance & Risk, Financial & Capital, Strategic Moves | Supply Chain |
| Healthcare / Pharma | Compliance & Risk, Technology & IP, Strategic Moves | Brand & Marketing |

## How to use

1. Identify the industry archetype from the user's scope answer (or infer from
   the competitor's main business).
2. Allocate ~70% of research time to high-priority dimensions.
3. For lower-priority dimensions, do a 5-minute scan only — capture anything
   surprising, otherwise mark `[brief scan, no deep analysis]`.
4. If the competitor spans multiple archetypes (e.g., a SaaS company that also
   sells hardware), merge the priority lists and mark dimensions that appear
   in both as "core" (always deep).

## When the user is in `quick` mode

In quick mode, restrict deep research to the top 4 high-priority dimensions
for the chosen archetype. The other dimensions get one-line placeholders:

```
[Quick mode — not pursued. Re-run in full mode for full coverage.]
```
